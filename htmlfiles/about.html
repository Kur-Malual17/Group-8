MobileCare South Sudan — Boat-Based Water Clinic

The Sudd Wetland is one of the world’s largest tropical wetlands (over 57,000 km²). Despite its rich ecology, more than a million people there face very limited access to healthcare and safe drinking water. MobileCare delivers vital healthcare, water purification, and health education via solar-powered floating clinics that serve remote wetland communities.

Problems We Address

  • Healthcare Access: Less than 30% of the population is within 5 km of a clinic. Seasonal flooding isolates communities and prevents land-based access.
  • High Mortality Rates: Maternal mortality and malaria remain leading causes of preventable death.
  • Unsafe Water: Many families use contaminated water, driving outbreaks of cholera and other illnesses.
  • Geographical Barriers: Vast waterways and seasonal floods make traditional land-based healthcare impractical.

Mission, Vision & Core Values

Mission

To provide life-saving healthcare, clean drinking water, and health education to remote communities in the Sudd Wetland through mobile, accessible, and sustainable medical solutions.

Vision

A South Sudan where every person, regardless of location, has access to essential healthcare and safe drinking water, fostering healthier communities and brighter futures.

Core Values

  • Accessibility: Reaching underserved communities.
  • Compassion: Delivering healthcare with empathy and respect.
  • Innovation: Using mobile clinics and telemedicine to overcome barriers.
  • Sustainability: Creating long-term impact through community involvement and education.
  • Integrity: Operating transparently and ethically.

Market Opportunity & Strategy

MobileCare targets the Sudd Wetland and similar wetland communities where access is limited. Our hybrid revenue model includes institutional contracts, CSR partnerships, small subsidized fees, telemedicine services, and grants — a balanced mix to ensure sustainability while maintaining affordability for communities.

For the initial phase, we focus on high-need communities and expand through partnerships, digital health integration, and careful scaling. Impact metrics include reduced maternal mortality, malaria incidence, and improved access to clean water.
